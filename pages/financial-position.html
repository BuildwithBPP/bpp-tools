3 · Key Ratios, in plain English
the teaching section
Each card: the number, whether it is healthy, what it means, and why it matters for BPP specifically. Built for anyone, no finance background needed.
~4.4
Runway (months)
watch
The most important number here. If revenue stopped tomorrow, cash covers about 4.4 months of operating costs (~$1,755/mo).
Every other number below looks great largely because the company is small and debt-free. Runway is the one that tells you how much breathing room you actually have. Build the cash reserve and this climbs.
11.22
Current ratio
healthy
For every $1 BPP owes in the near term, it has $11.22 available to cover it. Healthy for most businesses is 1.5 to 3.
Honest read: this is sky-high because liabilities are tiny, not because we are flush with cash. Don't let it create false comfort. It just means there is almost nothing to owe.
0.10
Debt-to-equity
low risk
For every $1 the owners have in the business, BPP owes about 10 cents. Essentially unleveraged.
No loans, no notes, just a credit card balance and the prior-owner payable. Low risk. It also means no borrowed money is working to grow the business, which is a choice, not a law.
$9.0K
Working capital
positive
What would be left if BPP paid off every short-term obligation today: $9,017.
This is the real operating cushion. Positive and clean. It is the same figure as equity right now because the balance sheet is almost entirely current assets.
57.3%
Net revenue retention
needs work
Of the revenue last year's clients gave us, we kept 57 cents on the dollar this year. Above 100% means clients grow with you; below means they shrink or leave.
This is the churn story in one number. It is why growth feels like running to stay in place. Fixing it is the retainer / recurring-revenue conversation.
40%
Logo retention
needs work
Of 5 paying clients last year, 2 are still paying this year. Three went to zero.
Revenue is up 36% YoY, but that is new clients replacing lost ones, not the same clients compounding. Keeping clients is cheaper than winning new ones every year.
71.2%

watch
The three biggest clients make up 71% of all revenue this year.
Lose one and you lose roughly a fifth of the business overnight. Not a crisis at this size, but a reason to keep the pipeline full and diversify the book.

8 · Data Quality & Open Items
read before trusting any single QuickBooks figure
The QuickBooks connector overstates its own summaries
Every hierarchical report double- or triple-counts sub-customers in its summary block. A/R aging reads $5,108 vs a true $1,793 (2.85x); sales-by-customer reads $20,768 vs $14,638; sales-by-product reads $57,833 vs $14,638. Every figure on this board was deduplicated by hand. The balance sheet is the one clean report and its A/R matched the corrected number exactly.
Open item 1 · undeposited funds
$444.15 sits in “Payments to Deposit,” and there is $444.15 of client credit balances. Almost certainly the same money: recorded but not deposited or applied. Clear it.
Open item 2 · net income mismatch
The P&L reports YTD net income of $3,287.00; the balance sheet reports $2,463.21. An $823.79 gap, possibly a fiscal-year setting. Resolve before the Q2 review.
