Operating Model

Two Types of Work. One Board.

We run our internal work as a Scrumban hybrid. Project work moves on a 3-week sprint cadence. Run-the-business work flows continuously in its own lane. Same board, two rhythms, no mixed signals. This is the operating model for the BPP Internal board going forward.

Board Layout
Groups
Operations
Product Backlog
Sprint Backlog
Done

Read top to bottom. The Operations lane (gold) is continuous flow. Product Backlog through Done is the sprint pipeline. Both end in the same Done group at completion; ops work that recurs never leaves the Operations lane.

The Two Types of Work
Sprint Work
Project work, committed

Real project work the team commits to and ships inside a 3-week sprint. Sized in story points. Tracked for velocity over time.

Examples Build a new service offering page. Draft a pricing deck. Set up a client onboarding template. Build the case study display page.

Lives in: Product Backlog until pulled into Sprint Backlog, then moves to Done at completion.

Operations Work
Run-the-business, continuous

The day-to-day stuff that just needs to happen. No story points. Never pulled into sprints. Splits into Recurring (repeats on a cadence) and Ad-hoc (one-off requests).

Examples Send Lois Marketing invoice. Weekly LinkedIn post. Renew Microsoft 365. Update Stripe payment method. Outreach follow-up.

Lives in: Operations lane. Recurring items stay there forever. Ad-hoc items move to Done on completion.

How to Know Which Lane

Five gates, top to bottom. The first one that fires wins.

1
Under 2 minutes? Faster to finish than to write, point, and track a card?
→
Just do it
2
Repeats on a rhythm (weekly, monthly, quarterly), or every time an event happens?
→
Recurring Ops
3
Interrupt-driven, can't wait for planning, or just needs doing with nothing to hand off?
→
Ad-hoc Ops
4
Plannable and it ships an artifact someone reviews (page, deck, tool, doc, system)?
→
Sprint
5
Tie-breaker: moves the business forward is Sprint, keeps the lights on is Ops.
→
Sprint

Tests (skill, plugin, workflow): testing a build you are shipping rides along as part of its Definition of Done, not a separate card. A one-time standalone check is Ad-hoc Ops, a repeating health check is Recurring Ops. Compound items split: the one-time build goes to Sprint, the ongoing chore it creates becomes a Recurring Ops card. Anti-pattern: story points on an errand means it belongs in Ops, not the backlog.

The Six Rules

Non-negotiables for the board to actually work.

RULE 01
Tag Work Type on every item
Sprint, Recurring Ops, or Ad-hoc Ops. Required on every card, both lanes. Sprint velocity is a filter on this column, and the Done-flow automations depend on it. An untyped item is invisible to both.
RULE 02
No story points on Ops items
Story Points measure sprint capacity. Ops work does not consume that capacity. Leave the column blank for Recurring and Ad-hoc Ops.
RULE 03
No Ops items in Sprint groups
If you find a Recurring or Ad-hoc Ops item in Product Backlog or Sprint Backlog, move it to the Operations lane. Sprint groups are for project work only.
RULE 04
WIP limit covers both lanes
Two items In Progress per person, total. A sprint item plus an ops item counts as two. Finish things before starting more.
RULE 05
Recurring lives as one card
The board auto-resets a recurring item when you mark it Done. Do not spawn a new card per occurrence. One card, forever.
RULE 06
Every item has an owner
Both lanes. No orphan tasks. If nobody owns it, it does not belong on the board.
The Done Flow

What happens when you mark something Done.

Work Type What happens at Done
Sprint Automation moves the item to the Done group. Sits there until the Saturday sprint review, when the whole Done group is archived.
Recurring Ops Automation resets Status to Not Started and advances the Due Date by the Recurrence interval. The card never leaves the Operations lane.
Ad-hoc Ops Automation moves the item to the Done group (same place Sprint items go). Archived as part of the whole Done group at the Saturday sprint review.
Why one Done group, not "Done (Sprint X)": the Sprint dropdown column already labels every item with its sprint, and Work Type distinguishes Sprint from Ops. So sprint velocity is a filter on the Done group, not a property of the group name. One generic Done group is simpler, and it gives Ad-hoc Ops a clean "disappears from the active lane" behavior.
What This Changes For Each Person

Read your card, then the other two.

Daunte
Finance & Operations · AI Workforce & Tech
Most of your Finance & Operations recurring work (bookkeeping, financial reports, statement reconciliation) becomes Recurring Ops. One card per task, with a Recurrence cadence set. The card resets itself when you mark it Done.
Kenny
Sales & Business Development · Marketing & Content
Outreach lives in HubSpot, not Monday. The Operations lane on this board picks up your marketing recurring work (weekly LinkedIn post, monthly email) and pipeline admin (referral follow-ups, partnership renewal checks). Sprint work is building new playbooks, sales assets, and content pieces.
Eli
Client Delivery & Design
Project-scale design and client delivery work goes to Sprint and gets story-pointed (a new service page, a deck redesign, a client deliverable revision). Recurring touches like brand asset upkeep or template maintenance go to the Operations lane.
When We Check Back In
Sprint 3 retrospective.
What we look at:
  • Is sprint planning faster than it was in Sprint 1?
  • Does sprint velocity reflect only sprint work, no ops contamination?
  • Are people using the main board for daily flow, or still going to "By Department" first?
  • Is the Operations lane staying under about 25 active items?
If something is not working, flag it at the Saturday ops meeting and we adjust.
