Template Feedback — Our Position

Kenny raised four concerns about the updated sequence templates in the team chat. Here's where we stand on each — what we agree with, where we push back, and the reasoning.

✅ We Agree — Action Items

Dynamic tokens missing from subject line — adding {{first}} as prefix

Kenny is right that the token was dropped. We're adding {{first}} as a lowercase prefix to the subject line across all template steps — e.g., "keleisha, think we might work with the same people". This keeps the agreed-upon lowercase, conversational style while adding personalization. Personalized subject lines increase open rates by ~22% vs. generic ones.[1]

✅ Confirmed: Update subject lines in all sequence steps to {{first}}, [existing subject] before next enrollment batch.

🟡 Clarification — Not a Disagreement

Why the signature was removed — and the correct fix

When Eli tested the updated templates, HubSpot automatically pulled his personal inbox signature and appended it to the email. This is by design — HubSpot sequences send from the connected inbox and auto-append the sender's email signature at the bottom of every step.[8]

If a signature block is also built into the template body, the recipient sees two signatures — one from the template and one auto-pulled from the inbox. That looks like a broken automated email. Eli was correct to remove it from the template.

The fix is not to put a signature in the template — it's to make sure the sender's signature in HubSpot's email integration settings is clean and professional. That auto-appended signature is what contacts see.

Action: Kenny (as admin) verifies the connected sender's HubSpot email signature (Settings → Email Integrations → Email Signature) is set up correctly.

📊 Respectfully Disagree — Research-Backed

Email length — short is intentional

Boomerang analyzed 40 million emails and found the highest response rates in the 50–125 word range.[2] Woodpecker's B2B cold email benchmark confirms short 1–3 paragraph emails outperform longer ones for reply rate.[3] Our own client acquisition strategy explicitly calls for precision-only cold email, never spray.[4]

Step 1 is a conversation opener, not a pitch. The full value proposition builds across all 6 steps over 18 days. A long Step 1 email reads like a brochure — it gets deleted, not replied to.

Position: Keep short format for all automated steps. Length is for personal follow-ups after a reply.

Subject line casing — lowercase is deliberate

Yesware's B2B sales email analysis shows casual, lowercase subject lines consistently outperform formal title-case ones in open rate.[5] HubSpot A/B tests show lowercase feels peer-to-peer rather than promotional — the goal in cold outreach.[6] Gmail also uses title case and punctuation density as signals to route email to the Promotions tab — lowercase keeps us in Primary.[7]

The subject line's only job is to get the email opened. Looking personal is the goal. The email body does the professional work.

Position: Keep informal lowercase subject lines across all automated sequence steps.
