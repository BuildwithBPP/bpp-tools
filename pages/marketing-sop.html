Why This Guide Exists

BPP’s content operation runs across three tools: Notion, Google Drive, and Metricool. The problem isn’t using any one of them — it’s knowing how they talk to each other and what you’re supposed to do at each step. This SOP is that map.

Part 1 — The System

01 The Three Tools & How They ConnectThe big picture before the detail

Every piece of BPP content flows through three tools. Understanding what each one owns — and what it does not own — is the foundation of the whole system.

Tool 1

Notion IdeaBank

The brain. Every idea starts here. Every status change happens here. This is the single source of truth for where every piece of content stands in the pipeline.

Tool 2

BPP Media Drive

The vault. All raw footage, review edits, and final cuts live here — organized by content day and by video. Nothing gets lost in a camera roll.

Tool 3

Metricool

The publisher. Final cuts get queued here for scheduling across platforms. Analytics on published content feed back into the Performance view in Notion.

The Rule

Notion is always first

No video gets filmed without an entry in Notion. No Drive folder gets created without a Notion entry it belongs to. Notion is the trigger for everything downstream.

The End-to-End Flow

💡 Notion IdeaBank — Idea created
Anyone adds a title, Format, and Pillar. Status = Idea. Under 30 seconds.
↓
✅ Notion — Content Lead reviews and schedules
Monthly review: Idea Vault combed oldest-first. Promising ideas become Approved. The best Approved ideas become Content Day with a script written beforehand.
↓
🎥 Content Day — Film + create Drive folder
On-camera talent films every Content Day idea. After each video: Drive folder created, raw footage uploaded to 01_Raw, Drive Link pasted into Notion, status flipped to Post-Production.
↓
✂️ Drive → Editor — Post-production
Editor opens the With Editor view in Notion, clicks the Drive Link to access raw footage, cuts and adds captions, saves the review cut to 02_Review. Creator approves, final goes to 03_Finals.
↓
📅 Metricool — Scheduled
Publisher takes the final cut from 03_Finals, queues it in Metricool with caption and publish date. Notion status flips to Scheduled.
↓
🟢 Published — Results logged in Notion
Once live, status flips to Published. About 30 days later, the Content Lead opens the Performance view, fills in Views, and sets the Result (Hit / TBD / Miss). Patterns inform next month’s approvals.

Part 2 — The Notion IdeaBank

02 Understanding the IdeaBankOne database · Multiple views · One flow

The Notion IdeaBank is a single database with multiple views — each one a different filtered window on the same data. You don’t move entries from one place to another. You change the Status field, and the entry automatically appears in the right view.

The mental model

Think of it like a whiteboard with sticky notes. The sticky notes (entries) don’t move around — you flip the color (Status). Each view is a window that only shows certain colors. Change the color, and the note appears in a new window automatically.

The IdeaBank covers all content formats — Reels, Carousels, LinkedIn Posts, Newsletters, Blog Articles. The Format property tells each entry what kind of content it is. Most views are designed for video content; for non-video formats, see the Content by Format section.

03 View 1 — Pipeline BoardDefault view · Full bird’s-eye picture

📋
Pipeline Board
Kanban · All active statuses · Default view
Everyone
Who uses it: Everyone
The Pipeline Board is your full-picture view of every piece of content in the system. It’s a Kanban board — ideas organized into columns by Status. This is the first view you see when you open the IdeaBank and the best place to understand what’s happening across the whole pipeline at a glance.

What you see

Each column = one Status. Cards show the idea’s title, Format, Pillar, and owner. From left to right, the columns follow the content lifecycle:

Idea → Approved → Content Day → Post-Production → Scheduled → Published

Parked ideas are hidden from this view by default. After a content day, status moves directly from Content Day to Post-Production once raw footage is uploaded to Drive.

If you see a “No Status” column

Some older IdeaBank entries were created before the pipeline was formalized and never had a status assigned. If you see a “No Status” column on the Pipeline Board, open each entry in that column and set Status to Idea. The board should only ever show the 7 named statuses. Going forward: every new entry must have Status = Idea set at the moment it’s created — never save a new entry with no status.

When to use it

  • Before a content meeting — spot where content is bottlenecked (too many Approved? Nothing in Post-Production?)
  • Quick status check — is anything stuck in the same column for two weeks?
  • Finding a specific piece — scan by column rather than searching the full database

Step by step

  1. Open the Notion IdeaBank. Pipeline Board should be selected as the default view.
  2. If you land on a different view, click the view name in the top-left of the database and select Pipeline Board.
  3. Scroll left and right to see all status columns. Click any card to open the full entry.
  4. To move an entry: open the card, click the Status field, and select the new status. The card will immediately move to the correct column. You can also drag cards between columns.

04 View 2 — Idea VaultRaw ideas · Content Lead’s review surface

🏦
Idea Vault
Filter: Status = Idea · Sorted oldest → newest
Anyone (add) · Kenny (review)
Who uses it: Anyone (to add) · Content Lead Kenny (to review)
The Idea Vault is where raw ideas live before anyone has decided if they’re worth making. It’s also where the Content Lead runs the monthly review — working through every idea oldest-first and making exactly one decision per entry: Promote, Park, or Delete. Nothing leaves a review session still at Idea status.

Adding a new idea (anyone on the team)

  1. Open the IdeaBank and click to the Idea Vault view.
  2. Click New at the top of the table or at the bottom of the list.
  3. Fill in three fields: Title (write it like a social post hook), Format (Reel, Carousel, Newsletter, etc.), Pillar.
  4. Leave Status = Idea. Don’t touch anything else. The whole point is speed — 30 seconds or less per idea.
Don’t judge ideas when you add them Just capture them. The hook doesn’t have to be perfect. The Content Lead will evaluate them in the monthly review — your job is to get the idea in the database before you forget it.

Monthly review (Content Lead)

Once a month, the week before content day, the Content Lead opens the Idea Vault and works through every entry oldest-first. Every entry at Status = Idea gets one of three decisions:

Promote → Approved
The team wants to make this. It serves a Pillar and has a clear hook. Flip to Approved. It’s now eligible for Content Day in the next cycle.
Park → Parked
Good concept, wrong season. Flip to Parked. It disappears from all active views and gets reviewed again at the quarterly purge.
Delete
Not good, already covered, or will never be made. Delete it. The bar: “Would we add this today if it wasn’t already here?” No → delete it.
Nothing leaves the session at “Idea”

This is the rule. Block 30 minutes, work all the way through the list, and don’t close the tab until every Idea-status entry has a decision.

05 View 3 — Monthly LineupContent day staging area · Content Day ideas only

📅
Monthly Lineup
Filter: Status = Content Day · Used on content day
Content Lead + On-Camera
Who uses it: Content Lead (to build) · On-Camera talent (on content day)
The Monthly Lineup is the “ready to film” view. It only shows entries with Status = Content Day — ideas that have been approved, scripted, and selected for the upcoming content day. This is the view that’s open on a phone or second screen during filming.

Building the lineup (Content Lead — week before content day)

  1. Open the Idea Vault and review all Approved entries. Pick the strongest 8–12 for the upcoming content day. Consider Format variety, Pillar balance, and any time-sensitive topics.
  2. For each selected entry, run the /content-script skill in Claude Code to generate the camera-ready script (see Section 11). Paste the output into the Notion page body.
  3. Change each entry’s Status from Approved → Content Day. It will now appear in the Monthly Lineup.
Content Day is the gate

Nothing gets filmed that isn’t at Content Day status. If a great idea comes up on content day that wasn’t in the lineup, add a new Notion entry, write the script, and flip it to Content Day before filming it. Otherwise it doesn’t exist in the system.

Using the lineup on content day (on-camera talent)

  1. Open the IdeaBank and switch to Monthly Lineup.
  2. You’ll see every Content Day idea for today. Click any card to open the full entry and review the hook and script before filming.
  3. Film each video using H-V-CTA-R: Hook (first 3–7 seconds) → Value (3 key points) → CTA (one clear ask) → Reset (repeat the hook to close the loop).
  4. After filming each video: create the Drive folder, upload raw footage, paste the Drive Link into Notion, and flip status to Post-Production. Do this per video — not as a batch at the end of the day.

06 View 4 — With EditorPost-production queue · Footage ready to edit

✂️
With Editor
Filter: Status = Post-Production · Shows Drive Link property
Editor
Who uses it: On-Camera (handoff) · Editor (post-production)
With Editor is the editor’s queue. It only shows entries with a Drive Link attached and Status = Post-Production. The editor opens this view, sees what’s waiting, clicks the Drive Link directly to the raw footage, edits, and saves the review cut. No guessing where the files are. No messages asking “did you upload yet?”

Handing off to the editor (after filming each video)

  1. Create the Drive folder inside today’s content day folder. Naming convention: [##]_(FirstName) - Exact Notion Title. Inside it, create three subfolders: 01_Raw, 02_Review, 03_Finals.
  2. Transfer raw footage from your camera to 01_Raw.
  3. Copy the Drive link to the per-video folder — the individual video folder, not the content day parent folder.
  4. In Notion, open the entry, paste the link into the Drive Link property, and flip Status to Post-Production. The entry now appears in the With Editor view.

Working in the With Editor view (editor)

  1. Open the IdeaBank and switch to With Editor.
  2. Every entry ready for editing appears here. The Drive Link is visible in the table without opening individual cards.
  3. Click the Drive Link for your current piece. It opens the per-video folder directly — raw footage is in 01_Raw.
  4. Edit: cuts, captions, music, color grade. Export the review file using the naming convention: BPP_[YYYY-MM-DD]_[short-title]_[platform].mp4. Save to 02_Review — not to Finals. Thumbnail options also go in 02_Review.
  5. Notify the creator (Slack, text, or tag in Notion) that the review cut is ready.

Review and approval (creator)

  1. Open the Drive Link and go to 02_Review. Watch the edit.
  2. If approved: move the final file to 03_Finals. Flip Status to Scheduled.
  3. If changes needed: leave it in 02_Review and send notes to the editor. The entry stays at Post-Production until approved.
Why 02_Review exists

03_Finals is clean. Only approved, ready-to-publish files live there. 02_Review is the working layer — rough cuts, thumbnail options, anything that hasn’t passed review yet. This keeps the publisher from accidentally uploading a rough cut to Metricool.

07 View 5 — Publishing QueuePublisher’s surface · Ready to go live

🚀
Publishing Queue
Filter: Status = Scheduled · Auto-exits when flipped to Published
Publisher
Who uses it: Publisher
The Publishing Queue shows everything that’s been edited, approved, and is waiting to go live. The publisher uses this to pull final cuts from Drive and queue them in Metricool. Once a piece goes live and status flips to Published, it automatically exits this view.

Publishing a piece step by step

  1. Open Publishing Queue to see everything at Status = Scheduled.
  2. Click into an entry. Open the Drive Link to navigate to the per-video folder. Open 03_Finals to find the approved file.
  3. Download the final file. Open Metricool, create a new post, upload the file, write the caption, select platforms, and set the publish date and time. (See Section 09 for full Metricool instructions.)
  4. When the content goes live, go back to Notion and flip Status to Published. The entry automatically exits this view.
  5. About 30 days later, fill in the Result field: Hit (outperformed expectations), TBD (still unclear), or Miss (underperformed). This is the performance signal for future approvals.
Where does published content go?

Once flipped to Published, the entry exits the Publishing Queue and moves to the Pipeline Board’s Published column. It also appears in the Performance view (Section 08) where the 30-day retro happens. Nothing is lost — it just moves to a different view.

08 View 6 — PerformancePost-publish archive · Monthly pattern review

📊
Performance
Filter: Status = Published · Views + Result properties
Content Lead
Who uses it: Content Lead (monthly retro)
The Performance view is the published content archive. Metricool is the primary analytics tool — it gives you the PDF report with reach, plays, and engagement. This view exists to log one-line results per entry so you can spot patterns over time: which topics, formats, and hooks perform?

The monthly retro (3 steps)

  1. Pull the Metricool PDF for the month (Analytics tab → select date range → Download PDF). This is your raw data source.
  2. Open Performance in Notion. For each published entry from the last content day, fill in Views with the 30-day reach number, and set Result: Hit / TBD / Miss.
  3. Look for patterns: which Format, Pillar, or hook type is consistently a Hit? Carry that signal into next month’s Idea Vault approvals.
Result is for learning, not judgment

A Miss isn’t a failure — it’s data. Mark it honestly. Over three months you’ll see patterns that make Content Day decisions much smarter.

Schedule View: use Metricool instead

The original SOP included a Notion calendar (Schedule View) for tracking publish dates. Metricool already has a planner calendar that shows your full publishing schedule — maintaining a second calendar in Notion creates two sources of truth. Use Metricool’s built-in planner as your editorial calendar. See Section 09 for how to use it.

Part 3 — Metricool

09 Metricool — Scheduling & AnalyticsWhere finals get published · Where results get tracked

Metricool is BPP’s publishing and analytics hub. Every final video goes through here before it hits Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Facebook, or YouTube. This section covers the full Metricool workflow: connecting your platforms, scheduling a post, and reading the analytics PDF.

📊
Metricool
app.metricool.com · BPP team account
Publisher
Who uses it: Publisher · Content Lead (analytics)
Metricool replaces juggling native apps for each platform. You upload once, choose your platforms, write the caption, pick a publish time, and it handles delivery. The analytics tab pulls performance data across all platforms into one place so you don’t have to check Instagram, TikTok, and LinkedIn separately.
1

Connect your platforms (one-time setup)

Go to app.metricool.com → Settings (gear icon) → Social Networks. Connect each platform BPP uses:

  • Instagram — connect via Facebook login (requires Business or Creator account)
  • TikTok — connect via TikTok login
  • LinkedIn — connect BPP’s Company Page
  • Facebook — connect the BPP Facebook Page
  • YouTube — connect via Google account (for long-form only)

Each platform needs to be re-authorized every 60–90 days due to token expiration. If a scheduled post fails to publish, check that the platform is still connected.

2

Schedule a new post

From the Metricool dashboard:

  • Click Create Post (or click any date in the Planner calendar)
  • Upload the final video file from 03_Finals in BPP Media Drive (download it to your computer first, then upload)
  • Select which platforms to post to (checkboxes across the top of the composer)
  • Write the caption in the main text field. If you’re posting to multiple platforms, each gets its own caption tab — customize the LinkedIn version for a professional tone
  • Set the publish date and time, or click Best Time to let Metricool suggest the optimal slot based on your audience’s past engagement
  • Click Schedule

After scheduling, go back to Notion and flip the entry’s Status to Scheduled to match.

3

The Planner — your editorial calendar

Click Planner in the left nav. You’ll see a calendar view of everything scheduled. This is your publishing schedule — use it to:

  • Check for gaps (nothing scheduled for a week) or clusters (three posts on the same day)
  • Drag-and-drop to reschedule without re-uploading
  • Click any post to edit the caption or platforms before it goes live

This replaces the need for a schedule view in Notion. Metricool is the authoritative calendar for what’s publishing and when.

4

Pulling the analytics PDF

About 30 days after a content day, pull the analytics PDF for the monthly retro:

  • Go to Analytics in the left nav
  • Select the date range covering your last content batch
  • Scroll down to find per-post stats (reach, plays, saves, profile visits, engagement rate)
  • Click Download PDF (top-right of the Analytics page)

The PDF gives you a summary per platform plus individual post stats. Use the per-post play counts to fill in the Views field in Notion’s Performance view.

5

The feedback loop back to Notion

After pulling the PDF:

  • Open the Performance view in Notion
  • For each published entry, fill in Views (plays from Metricool) and set Result (Hit / TBD / Miss)
  • The patterns that emerge from 3+ months of Results directly inform what gets Approved in the Idea Vault — this is how the content gets smarter over time

Part 5 — Script Workflow

11 The Content Script WorkflowWrite the script before you walk in front of the camera

The bottleneck on content day is rarely ideas — it’s walking in front of the camera without a locked hook and a clear structure. BPP has two Claude Code skills built to solve this. Use them during the week before content day, after an entry moves to Content Day status.

Primary skill: /content-script

This is the day-to-day script generator. Run it for every piece in the Monthly Lineup before content day starts.

BPP Skill — Day-to-Day
/content-script
Turns a Notion IdeaBank entry into a camera-ready script. Works for Reels, Carousels, and LinkedIn Posts using BPP brand voice automatically.
Mode 1 — H-V-CTA-R Batch Reel
Fast, punchy 60-second Reel script. Best for content day batches where you’re filming 8–12 pieces. Hook + 3 value bullets + CTA + Reset.
Mode 2 — 5-Part Pillar Piece
Deeper authority content up to 90 seconds. Hook → Context → Tension → Pivot → Payoff. For planned series or high-stakes topics.
Carousel Mode
Slide-by-slide copy: Hook slide → Pain → Step 1/2/3 → Example → CTA. Includes design notes and export specs.
Output Includes
Full script, on-screen text cues for the editor, export specs per platform, and suggested CTA keyword.

The H-V-CTA-R framework

Every BPP Reel script follows this four-part structure. The skill enforces it — but knowing it helps you deliver on camera:

H
Hook
0–7 seconds
Stops the scroll. ≤12 words. Start with a number, action verb, or bold claim. Repeat at the end.
V
Value
Majority of video
3 specific bullets, ~8–10 seconds each. Start each with a verb. Speak straight to camera.
CTA
Call to Action
10–15 seconds
One clear ask: “Comment [KEYWORD] if you want [outcome]. Or book a free consult — link in bio.”
R
Reset
5–10 seconds
Repeat the exact hook line from the opening. Closes the loop and reinforces the takeaway.

How to run it

  1. Open Claude Code and type /content-script (or /script)
  2. The skill will ask for: the idea title, Format (Reel / Carousel / LinkedIn Post), who’s on camera, Pillar, and optional CTA keyword
  3. It outputs a complete script formatted to paste directly into the Notion page body (Draft section)
  4. Paste the script into the entry. Confirm the CTA keyword is visible so the editor can add it to the on-screen text
  5. Flip the entry to Content Day if not already done

Strategic skill: /video-content-strategist

This is a different tool for a different job. Use it quarterly or when planning a new content direction — not for individual scripts.

BPP Skill — Quarterly / Strategic
/video-content-strategist
Channel strategy, 90-day content roadmaps, YouTube SEO keyword research, and repurposing pipelines. Turns one long-form asset into 10+ pieces across platforms.
Mode 1 — Strategy
Niche definition, content pillars, SEO keyword targets, 90-day launch plan.
Mode 2 — Script
Long-form YouTube or short-form scripts with chapters and B-roll notes.
Mode 3 — Repurpose
Atomize one blog post, webinar, or podcast into 10+ platform-adapted video assets.
Best for
Planning a new series, launching a YouTube channel, or turning an existing piece of content into a multi-platform campaign.

Part 6 — Content by Format

12 Content by FormatNot all content goes through the video pipeline

The IdeaBank tracks all BPP content types — not just video. The Format property on each entry determines which production path it follows. Video formats use the full pipeline (Drive + editor + Metricool). Written and designed formats skip the Drive and editor steps entirely.

Format types in the IdeaBank

Reel / Short Carousel LinkedIn Post Blog / Article Newsletter YouTube (Long-Form)

Production path by format

🎥
Reel / Short & YouTube
  1. Script with /content-script
  2. Film on content day
  3. Create Drive folder (01_Raw / 02_Review / 03_Finals)
  4. Editor edits, saves to 02_Review
  5. Creator approves, moves to 03_Finals
  6. Publisher queues in Metricool
🖼️
Carousel
  1. Script slides with /content-script (Carousel mode)
  2. Design slides in Canva using BPP brand template
  3. Export as image set (PNG)
  4. Schedule in Metricool as a carousel post

No Drive folder needed. Status: Idea → Approved → Content Day → Scheduled → Published.

📰
Newsletter
  1. Draft with /content-script (newsletter mode) or /content-production
  2. Review and edit in Claude Code or Google Docs
  3. Send via email platform (Mailchimp or equivalent)

No Drive folder. No Metricool. Status goes directly: Content Day → Published.

✍️
Blog / Article
  1. Outline and draft with /content-script or /content-production
  2. Edit and proof-read
  3. Publish to website (Webflow CMS or WordPress)

No Drive folder. No Metricool for delivery. Status: Content Day → Published.

Key rule: Drive + With Editor = video only

The BPP Media Drive and the With Editor view are exclusively for video content (Reels, Shorts, YouTube). Carousels, Newsletters, and Blog posts skip both. If a non-video entry shows up in the With Editor view, check that the Status was set correctly — it should only be at Post-Production if raw footage exists in Drive.

Approved finals

Only approved, ready-to-publish files. Publisher pulls from here to upload to Metricool. If it’s in 03_Finals, it’s been approved and is safe to publish.

Always use the exact Notion title Don’t shorten it or paraphrase it. If the Notion entry is titled “How We Used AI to Save 10 Hours This Week,” the folder is named exactly that. Exact match keeps everything traceable from Notion → Drive → editor.

Final export file naming

Format: BPP_[YYYY-MM-DD]_[short-title]_[platform].mp4

Example: BPP_2026-07-15_relationships-close-deals_IG.mp4

Platform codes: IG Instagram · TT TikTok · LI LinkedIn · YT YouTube Shorts

Linking Drive to Notion (the Drive Link property)

  1. In Google Drive, navigate to the per-video folder (e.g., 01_(Eli) - Project Management Isn’t a Tool/).
  2. Right-click the folder → Get link. Set to “Anyone with the link can view.”
  3. Copy the link.
  4. In Notion, open the corresponding entry and paste the link into the Drive Link property field.
Link to the per-video folder, not the content day folder

The Drive Link in Notion should point to the individual video’s folder — not to the parent date folder. The editor needs to land directly on the right footage, not dig through 12 videos to find theirs.

14 Content Day PlaybookStart to finish — everything in order

This is the full run-of-show for a BPP content day — from opening Notion in the morning to footage uploaded and the editor notified before you leave.

Week Before — Content Lead
Run the monthly Idea Vault review (Promote / Park / Delete every Idea-status entry). Select 8–12 for Content Day. Run /content-script for each and paste scripts into the Notion page body. Flip each to Content Day. Confirm the Monthly Lineup is fully populated with outlines before content day morning.
Morning of Content Day
On-camera talent opens Monthly Lineup and reads through all Content Day entries. Review each hook and script structure. You’re not memorizing — you’re making sure the direction is clear before the camera turns on.
During Filming
Film each Content Day idea using H-V-CTA-R: Hook (3–7 seconds) → Value (3 key points) → CTA (one clear ask) → Reset (repeat the hook). Keep Monthly Lineup open on a second screen or phone. Record multiple takes. Keep energy up — the editor needs options.
After Each Video (before filming the next)
Do the handoff immediately after each video — not at the end of the day:
1. Create the per-video Drive folder ([##]_(Name) - Title) with three subfolders: 01_Raw, 02_Review, 03_Finals.
2. Transfer raw footage from your camera to 01_Raw.
3. In Notion, paste the Drive link into the Drive Link field and flip Status to Post-Production.
Repeat for every video before moving to the next one.
End of Content Day
Every filmed entry should be at Status = Post-Production with a Drive Link. Monthly Lineup should be empty. If any ideas didn’t get filmed, flip them back to Approved. Notify the editor that footage is ready in Drive.
Editor’s Window
Editor opens With Editor view, works through each entry using the Drive Links. Edits and saves review cut to 02_Review (thumbnail options too). Notifies creator when each piece is ready for review. Creator approves and moves final to 03_Finals. Publisher queues in Metricool and flips status to Scheduled.
30 Days After Publishing
Content Lead pulls the Metricool analytics PDF. Opens Performance view in Notion. Fills in Views and Result for everything published from this content day. Patterns from this retro directly inform what gets Approved in the next monthly cycle.

16 Editorial RulesSeries · Stale ideas · Cadence

Series Rules

A series is a named campaign with a concrete plan — not just a theme. The rules:

  • Only activate a series when you have 3+ ideas planned. Create all 3+ Notion entries at once, tag them with the same Series name, and put them Content Day together.
  • Film as a batch. All ideas in a series get shot on the same content day and release in consecutive weeks.
  • Name it specifically. “Tools That Saved Us 10 Hours This Week” is a real campaign. A broad topic bucket is not.
  • Leave Series blank for standalone pieces. Don’t tag something as a series “in case it becomes one.”
  • Existing dormant series (1 min Biz Tip, Tools That Are 2 Good, Founder Psychology) stay in the dropdown but nothing should be tagged to them without a 3-part plan in hand.

Stale Ideas — Monthly Rule

Every Idea-status entry gets a decision every month. The rule: nothing leaves the monthly review session still at Idea status. Work through the Idea Vault oldest-first. Every entry gets Promoted, Parked, or Deleted before you close the tab.

Quarterly Hard Purge (Jan · Apr · Jul · Oct)

  • Review everything at Status = Parked. Ask: “If this wasn’t already in the database, would we add it today?”
  • No → delete it. Yes → move back to Idea for the next monthly cycle.
  • Anything Parked for 6+ months gets deleted automatically. No exceptions.
