Retrospective · 4Ls
Source: internal retro call, August 22, 2026
Client-specific behavioral detail is held in the client workspace record rather than published here. What follows is the pattern-level read.
Liked
Why before what. Reframing the build around what it specifically pays off for this owner, and how he would use it, moved him from polite to engaged. The turn landed 30 to 45 minutes into a session, roughly 40 to 60 percent of the way through.
Live demo as the teaching method. Answering the client's question by asking the system in front of him, out loud. That teaches prompting by doing. It is the most transferable thing that came out of the setup work.
The mid-project expectation reset. A second BPP voice having a separate conversation to realign expectations is what made the back half of the work land.
The financial clarity session. Closed a discovery-stage pain point out loud, in front of the client. Exactly what a closeout should be able to point back at.
The closeout package. Ten-slide deck, pre-call gates, per-slide objection handling and a scope sheet. Reusable as-is.
Learned
Set expectations before the action-plan conversation, not during it. The named top lesson. The miss was no agenda and no internal touch-base on what the other BPP voice had already told the client, so the room was not on the same page.
We assume prerequisite knowledge clients do not have. No free disk or RAM to run anything natively, no membership, and no mental model of what a local file even is. The same pattern appeared on the previous workspace setup at another client. This is the service, not one client.
Environment decides whether technical delivery lands. Sessions where the decision-maker sat at a table with the laptop worked. Sessions the client hosted in an informal setting, distracted and running long, transferred nothing. Relationship building survives that. Instruction does not.
The technical seat is not delegable mid-session. Handing the work down to an unprepared team member spends the session on login troubleshooting and transfers no knowledge.
Retainers sell easier at proposal than at closeout. Switching from delivery to upsell at the close is a hiccup for us and bad psychology for them.
Prompt engineering is a weak 2026 lead magnet. The models do the role framing themselves now, unprompted. Skills is the stronger training module and the stronger magnet.
Lacked
A pre-setup technical readiness check. Device specs, free disk, RAM, active subscription. None of it verified before the session was booked. All of it blocked the work.
A plain explanation of what running natively means. It took about 35 minutes, delivered twice, to land the idea that the model has to look at the file and the file has to be there to look at.
A "do not delete this" guardrail. Nothing told the client the workspace folder was the product.
In-the-moment objection handling. Self-identified. Reading the room live and answering resistance as it happens.
Meetings booked at kickoff. The client-side latency that drove the 6 was structural, not personal. This owner responds to a calendar invite and leaves texts and email unread.
An internal pre-brief before client-facing planning calls. Two BPP people carried two versions of the story into one conversation.
A trustworthy board. Two milestones shipped and nobody moved status. Second engagement in a row with this exact problem.
Longed-for
An AI workspace setup SOP. Prerequisites checklist, run-of-show, the standard explanations that actually work, and a one-page client-side "what this is and what not to touch."
A packaged Skills and prompting 101 module that doubles as a lead magnet and as the training we currently improvise.
A standing rule on setting. Where technical sessions happen, and who has to be in the seat when they do.
Watch tape as a routine. We record and transcribe everything already. Reviewing our own calls for weak CTAs and missed objections is a habit we talk about and do not run.
The setup scoped as a real deliverable with a definition of done, a price, and a line on the board.

Lessons We Carry Forward
5 numbered · mirrored in the Client Lessons Learned log
1
Gate every AI workspace setup behind a technical readiness check. The client had no free storage, no RAM headroom and no active membership, and the session ran anyway. It turned a productized setup into hours of unbilled labor. Verify device specs, free disk and an active subscription before the session goes on a calendar.
2
Teach what a local file is before teaching what the system does, then hand over a "do not delete" sheet. The client deleted his entire workspace folder believing it was a leftover download artifact, and it only surfaced at closeout during a rabbit chase over a skill that would not run. Recovery cost roughly 35 minutes of explanation on top of the troubleshooting time.
3
Book every meeting at kickoff. Client-side latency was the top driver of the 6 rating and it was structural. This owner respects a calendar invite and leaves texts and email unread for days. Send the full meeting set and take availability before the kickoff call ends. Applies immediately to every open engagement.
4
Technical delivery needs a technical setting, and the technical seat is not delegable. A session where the decision-maker is distracted and hands the laptop to an unprepared team member is not a working session. Reschedule it. Pushing through produces a client who cannot use what he bought, which is exactly what happened here.
5
Sell the retainer at proposal, not at closeout. An extra $250 on an $8,000 package is invisible while the client is already saying yes. The same $250 asked at the close is a fresh decision made after the buying energy is gone, and it forces us to switch from delivery mode into sales mode in front of them.
+ also: pre-brief internally before client-facing planning calls · move Monday status as work changes, this is the second engagement in a row where the board could not be trusted · scope and price the workspace setup as a real deliverable · Skills, not prompt engineering, is the 2026 training and lead-magnet play · give written termination notice whenever a contract calls for one.
