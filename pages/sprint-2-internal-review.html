Retrospective
What went well · What slowed us down · Action items
Observations derived from the board data above. Edit before the team retro adds its own voice.
What went well
The content engine is live. The full bi-weekly newsletter cadence shipped (internal + client) plus the website rollout plan. Marketing closed 5 of 7 items.
Delivery infrastructure landed. The Resource Page is public and the bpp-client-delivery plugin is built. Two 8-point Criticals done that standardize every future onboarding.
Eli ran at 84% of points. The highest-value infra shipped without slipping.
Lighter work closed cleanly. Every item at 3 points or under that got started, got finished.
What slowed us down
The Criticals slipped. 3 of 5 carryover items are Critical priority (Outreach, HubSpot, BPP Business Plan). The highest-priority revenue and positioning work is what carried.
We over-committed. 59 points planned, 30 delivered. The heavier items (avg ~5.8 pts) carried; the lighter ones closed.
Survey Page never started. It lost the capacity race to two 8-pt Criticals. Honest read: it should not have been committed without room for it.
Daunte's bandwidth was constrained. Outside work commitments limited his availability this sprint, which is the real driver behind the lower completion, not the work or the effort.
We weren't updating the board as we go. Status was not kept current during the sprint, so the review numbers may understate what got done. A review is only as trustworthy as the board behind it.
Action items for Sprint 3
Carry the 3 Criticals first. Outreach, HubSpot, and the BPP Business Plan lead Sprint 3 before any new scope.
Right-size the commitment. Plan ~30–35 points (our real Sprint 2 velocity), not 59. Plan to velocity, not optimism.
Protect Kenny's load. Cap his sprint near 8 points during ramp, or pair/redistribute one Critical.
Start-by date on every High and Critical. Survey Page sat at Not Started for three weeks. A start date surfaces that risk early.
Update the board as we go. Move status the moment work changes, throughout the sprint, not the night before review, so the next review's numbers hold up.
Commit against real availability. Plan each owner's load against their actual bandwidth for the sprint (Daunte's outside work, Kenny's ramp), not a full-time assumption.
