Retrospective
What went well · What slowed us down · Action items
Observations derived from the board data above, viewed with the benefit of hindsight from Sprint 2. Edit before the team retro adds its own voice.
What went well
A near-complete close. 18 of 19 committed items done, 49 of 54 points. The only miss was a single Critical that carried.
The operating system got built. GitHub repo + Pages dashboard + team training, Claude memory and context, the PM playbook, the ICP and service-delivery playbook, standardized onboarding and closeout decks. The infrastructure that made every later sprint faster.
Finance hygiene landed. Canceled redundant subscriptions (Webflow, Todoist), closed the Q1 review, reconciled rev-share, sent invoices.
Granular items closed cleanly. Most of the sprint was 1 to 3 point items, and all of them finished inside the window.
What slowed us down
A Critical carried, and kept carrying. Kenny's Update HubSpot Pipelines (5 pts) was the one miss. It pushed to Sprint 2, did not close there either, and is open into Sprint 3. The pipeline work has been the hardest item to land.
The bulk sat on two people. Daunte and Eli carried 18 of the 19 items. A strong result, but a single-point-of-failure risk, and Kenny's lone item is exactly the one that slipped.
No review or board-hygiene ritual yet. Sprint 1 had no formal retro and the board was not yet a live system, which is why this review is a backfill. That missing habit became Sprint 2's biggest lesson.
The work was inward-facing. Setup and internal foundation is far more controllable than client and sales work. The high completion rate set an expectation that the outward push of Sprint 2 could not match.
Action items it taught us
Keep estimates granular. Small items closed clean here; the heavier items that slipped in Sprint 2 proved the point. Keep work small enough to finish in the sprint.
Widen ownership. Spread the sprint beyond two people so no one is a single point of failure. Acted on in Sprint 2 by bringing Kenny in.
Stand up the review and board-hygiene ritual. Make the retro a standing habit and keep the board current as work moves, so reviews never have to be reconstructed again.
Plan to a realistic velocity when scope turns outward. Do not treat a near-perfect setup sprint as the standard for client-facing sprints. Plan to demonstrated pace, not the foundation-sprint high.
