Retrospective · 4Ls
Liked · Learned · Lacked · Longed-for
Internal team reflection. Honest read, not marketing copy.
Liked
Dual-entity firewall discipline held. Books, email, Google orgs, donor data versus youth data. The separation stayed clean the whole way through, and that discipline is exactly what makes the plan grant-ready.
Tool recommendations came from research, not a vendor list. The CRM answer ran through parallel research passes and named real nonprofit analogs as proof points instead of guessing.
Working sessions closed decisions. The June 11 website review settled the entity rename, the domain, the two-plan split, the ownership model, and a full set of copy changes in one sitting.
Learned
State incorporation is not federal exemption, and the gap costs twice. Once when nonprofit perks silently fail verification, and again when a public page carries a tax-status claim the organization cannot yet make.
Launch Pad breaks on dual-entity clients. The package prices one business plan. Two firewalled plans is close to double the work and does not fit inside a $200 credit.
A pre-revenue nonprofit in an IRS waiting period has almost no unblocked revenue levers. Donor marketing and Ad Grants are IRS-gated, grant writing was already outsourced, and the Form 1023 sat with their attorney. Do not manufacture a package to fill that gap.
Lacked
No 501(c)(3) verification gate. Florida incorporation plus an EIN got treated as federal exemption. Google for Nonprofits stalled mid-setup, and a false "registered 501(c)(3)" claim with the EIN sat live on a public donation page until the board caught it July 14.
Webflow platform churn. Built on the free plan, hit walls on custom code, native forms, and page creation, upgraded to Basic mid-build, then spent two sessions untangling hosting ownership at handoff.
Entity and naming churn. The 501(c)(3) was filed as HALO Pathways, not HACO Pathways, mid-engagement. Nav, footer, donate-page SEO, the workspace folder, and the logo prompt all carried the wrong spelling for weeks.
Timeline slipped twice. Closeout targeted July 17, moved to August 8, landed August 3. Root cause was a revision cycle opening on an ~80-page document with no review window budgeted in the original plan.
Longed-for
A compliance gate at intake. One checklist run before any build that answers what this organization can legally claim, sell, and solicit today.
A nonprofit and dual-entity package tier. Something priced for the real work instead of discounting a single-entity package to fit.
A pre-publish review for regulated claims. A gate that would have caught the tax-status language before it went live rather than three weeks after.
Action Plan · DAKI
Drop · Add · Keep · Improve
Each Add and Improve carries an owner and a "before the next engagement" trigger.
Drop
Fronting client hosting. BPP never puts its card on a client's site plan. Two sessions lost, a prepaid year stranded in the wrong workspace, and the client asked to pay for a year they believed they had already bought. Now codified in the Webflow Client Hosting SOP.
Starting nonprofit setup before the IRS letter. No Google for Nonprofits, TechSoup, donor tooling, or donation surface until the determination letter is physically in hand.
Taking card details verbally. The founders read a live card number, expiry, and CVC aloud on a recorded session. The automated digit scan missed it because the number came in four-digit chunks, and it had to be scrubbed by hand.
Add
A nonprofit compliance gate at intake. Determination letter status, state charitable-solicitation registration, what is legally claimable in public today, and Form 1023 scoped as a paid add-on. Owner: Eli · before the next 501(c)(3) engagement
A dual-entity and nonprofit package tier. Priced above Launch Pad to reflect two firewalled plans and the extra compliance work. Owner: Eli + Daunte · before the next nonprofit proposal
A legal review before anything client-facing publishes. Tax status, licensure, credentials, and solicitation language checked against evidence, not against what the client believes is true. Owner: Rodney (board) + Eli · every regulated client
Keep
Dual-entity firewall discipline. Separate the for-profit and nonprofit entities everywhere: books, email, Google orgs, CRM. Blending them muddies grant compliance and is expensive to unwind.
Research-backed tool recommendations. Answer tool questions with sourced research and real analogs, and reject options on a specific named constraint rather than on preference.
Working sessions that end in decisions. Run client sessions that close decisions with owners attached, not sessions that only gather feedback.
Improve
Budget revision windows into the timeline. Size the review window to the deliverable up front. An ~80-page plan needs weeks built in, not discovered after the closeout date is committed. Owner: Eli
Lock entity naming before assets carry it. Confirm the legal name from the filed documents first. Naming churn ripples into copy, SEO, domains, logos, and folder structure. Owner: Eli
Closeout date discipline. Treat the closeout date as a commitment with a named blocker list, not a rolling target. Two slips on one engagement is a signal even when each move is individually defensible. Owner: Eli
Verify handoff training live. Walk the client through with the guide open beside the real interface, so drift between the doc and the platform surfaces with the client present. Owner: Eli
Lessons We Carry Forward
Also logged in Client Lessons Learned
These mirror this client's entry in the cumulative Client Lessons Learned log →
1
Confirm the IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter before promising nonprofit perks and before making any public tax-status claim. Google, TechSoup, and the rest verify against IRS federal databases, so state incorporation plus an EIN fails. That gap cost twice here: the Google for Nonprofits setup stalled mid-flight, and a false "registered 501(c)(3)" claim with the EIN sat on a live public donation page until the board ordered it stripped on July 14. Soliciting in Florida also triggers an FDACS charitable-solicitation registration duty regardless of IRS status. Correct sequence: state formation and EIN, then Form 1023, then the determination letter, then perks and any public claim.
2
Sell the Form 1023 filing as a paid add-on for every 501(c)(3) client. The filing is a real prerequisite for all the nonprofit tech perks, the IRS process is slow, and an attorney charges far more than BPP needs to. Scoping it up front is both a genuine value-add and a margin line.
3
Never front a client's website hosting. Put their card on it from day one. BPP paid HALO's Webflow site plan as a convenience. That shortcut cost two working sessions, produced a client-facing moment where HALO was asked to pay for a year they believed they had already bought, and left a prepaid year stranded in the wrong workspace. It also meant taking card details verbally on a recorded call.
4
A prepaid site plan does not travel with a site transfer, and "transfer" is usually the wrong tool anyway. The workspace plan and the site plan are different things. When the goal is "the client can update their own site," the answer is a free content-editor seat. Reserve an ownership transfer for when the client must legally hold the account, and time it at renewal.
5
Dual-entity nonprofits need their own package tier. Launch Pad prices one business plan. HALO needed two firewalled plans, one for the LLC and one for the 501(c)(3), which roughly doubled the plan effort and was absorbed for a ~$200 credit. Price the structure, not just the deliverable count.
+ also: lock the legal entity name from the filed documents before any asset carries it · budget the revision window into the original timeline, sized to the deliverable · never collect payment details on a call.
