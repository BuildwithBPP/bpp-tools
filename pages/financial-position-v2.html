BPP is debt-free and owes no vendors. Almost everything it holds is cash and one client balance. Strong and clean, but small, so the number that matters most is runway, not the size of the balance sheet. Operating is ~$1,656 below the two-month buffer — collection, not the reserve, is the fix.

7Data quality & open itemsread before trusting any single QuickBooks figure
The QuickBooks connector overstates its own summaries
Every hierarchical report double- or triple-counts sub-customers: A/R aging reads $5,108 vs a true $1,793; sales-by-customer $20,768 vs $14,638; sales-by-product $57,833 vs $14,638. Every figure here was deduplicated by hand. The balance sheet is the one clean report, and its A/R matched the corrected number exactly.
Open item 1 · undeposited funds = unapplied A/R credits
$444.15 in Payments to Deposit is the same $444.15 of unapplied payments dragging A/R down: Stack All Profitsa client $424.15 (Sep 2025 overpayment) + four $5 “BPP” test payments (Jan 2026). Received, never deposited or applied. Clearing them fixes both the asset and the A/R.
Open item 2 · net income mismatch
P&L reports YTD net income $2,931.30; balance sheet reports $2,107.51. The same $823.79 gap as last week — structural, not drift. Likely a fiscal-year setting. Resolve before the next review.
Open item 3 · revenue not tied to invoices
$414.97 of P&L income isn't tied to a customer invoice ($20 unapplied cash, $20 uncategorized, ~$375 direct-to-income). This is why the client and product tables sum to $14,637.73 while P&L revenue is $15,052.70. Tie the postings to customers in QB and the reports agree.
