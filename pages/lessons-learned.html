Sprint 2 — Internal
Internal Sprint
May 18 – Jun 7, 2026 · 7 of 12 items done · 30 of 59 points · Full review →
1
Plan to velocity, not optimism. We committed 59 points and delivered 30. Sprint 3 should plan to ~30–35, our actual demonstrated pace.
2
Criticals slip when they are also the heaviest items. The three Criticals that carried were all 5–8 points. Protect top-priority work by sequencing it first and keeping it small enough to finish.
3
Put a start-by date on every High and Critical. The Survey Page sat at Not Started for the whole sprint. A start date would have surfaced the capacity gap in week one.
4
Match load to capacity during a ramp. Kenny held two Criticals (13 pts) while still ramping; both carried. Cap a ramping owner near 8 points or pair on the heavy item.
5
Commit against real availability. Daunte's bandwidth was constrained by outside work this sprint. Plan each owner's load against their actual availability, not a full-time assumption.
6
Update the board as we go, not before the review. Status was not kept current during the sprint, so the numbers may understate what got done. Move status the moment work changes, throughout the sprint. A review is only as trustworthy as the board behind it.
Sprint 1 — Internal
Internal Sprint
Apr 7 – May 4, 2026 · 18 of 19 items done · 49 of 54 points · backfilled Jun 8, 2026 · Full review →
1
A foundation sprint of internal setup is highly completable. We closed 18 of 19. Treat that as a setup-sprint baseline, not the standard to expect once scope turns outward to clients and sales.
2
Build the operating system first. Sprint 1 stood up the GitHub repo and Pages dashboard, Claude memory and context, the PM playbook, the ICP and service-delivery playbook, and standardized onboarding and closeout decks. That infrastructure made every later sprint faster.
3
Granular estimates close clean. Most items were 1 to 3 points and finished inside the window. Keep work small enough to finish in the sprint.
4
A Critical can hide in a single owner's lane. Kenny's lone item, Update HubSpot Pipelines, was the only miss, and it has now carried across Sprint 1, 2, and into 3. Sequence and protect Criticals, and do not leave one owner single-threaded on one.
5
Stand up the review and board-hygiene ritual early. Sprint 1 had no formal retro and the board was not yet a live system, which is why it had to be reconstructed after the fact. That missing habit became Sprint 2's biggest lesson.
