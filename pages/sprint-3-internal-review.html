Retrospective
What went well · What slowed us down · Action items
Observations reconstructed from the board, HubSpot, the team inbox, and the Jul 13 + Jul 18 team decisions. Edit before the team retro adds its own voice.
What went well
We ran the experiment to the end. Extending to Jul 18 to see real sequence results, instead of closing on a guess, was the right call. We now have data, not opinions, about solo-run outreach.
The referral list over-delivered. The "list 15" item shipped as 30 sourced and enrolled. Sourcing was never the bottleneck.
The sprint pointed at the north star. Half the committed items were Sales & BD. The team put its weight where revenue is.
Daunte named the real blocker. Raising the "deadlines set without checking availability" root cause in the open is how the planning process actually improves.
The sprint produced two durable decisions. The revenue-only sprint rule and the hire-and-contract model both came out of this cycle. That is compounding value.
What slowed us down
Outreach converted thin. A full referral + cold cycle produced ~1 reply and no sprint close. Two people cannot run high-volume outreach and deliver client work at the same time.
Daunte's 3 Criticals carried a second sprint. HubSpot Pipelines, BPP Business Plan, and Free Resources all carried from Sprint 2 and stalled again, then were reassigned Jul 13. A two-sprint carry means the commitment was never real.
Four items never started. SMS tool, retainer packages, Xtremely Clean case study, and FB groups sat at Not Started the whole sprint. We committed more than we had capacity for, again.
The board went stale on Jun 29. This was a named Sprint 2 action item and it recurred. A review is only as trustworthy as the board behind it, and this one was not maintained.
A ~5-week sprint hid the slippage. Extending is fine once, for a reason. But a long sprint with a frozen board made it easy to lose track of what was actually moving.
Action items for Sprint 4
Only revenue or lead work enters a sprint. Adopt the Jul 13 rule as the hard gate. Internal tooling and ops move to a separate operations lane, out of the sprint burndown.
Staff outreach as a role, not a side task. Execute the Jul 18 decision: hire a sales person to own the outreach motion and contract out delivery so the founders are not the bottleneck.
Plan against real availability. Before assigning a deadline, confirm the owner's actual bandwidth for the sprint. No more full-time assumptions on part-time capacity.
No item carries twice. If something carries a second sprint, it gets reassigned, re-scoped, or killed at planning, not re-committed to the same owner.
Two-week sprints, board updated as we go. Return to a tight cadence and move status the moment work changes. If the board cannot be trusted, cancel the review until it can.
Account for Eli's new schedule. The Pinellas job (started Jul 20) puts Eli on nights and weekends for BPP. Plan his load accordingly and let contractors carry delivery.
