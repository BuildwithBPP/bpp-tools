Key Terms Used in This Synthesis

DRI · Directly Responsible Individual
The single named person accountable for a function. Decisions in their lane move through them. Not the same as "does all the work" but the one who owns the outcome.
Backup
The named second. Steps in if DRI is unavailable. Cross-trained over time so the function never has a single point of failure.
Async-DACI
A decision pattern. Owner proposes a decision in writing. Others have until end of day to object. Silence equals approval. Replaces in-meeting "you guys good?" rubber-stamping.
AI Worker
A Claude Code plugin or MCP automation that handles repeatable parts of a function. Each AI worker has a human DRI, treated as a real seat on the chart.
MCP · Model Context Protocol
The connection layer between Claude and external tools (Outlook, HubSpot, Monday, Notion). MCPs let AI workers do real work, not just give advice.
Single Point of Failure
A function only one person knows how to do. If they're out, the work stops. Rodney's headline observation: BPP has too many of these today.

What changed when we walked out of that room

A 2h 51m session with all three owners in person at USF and Rodney remote on Teams. The chart got ratified, owners got named, and a handful of net-new operating norms came out of the conversation. This banner is the short version. The chart, themes, and tables below have been updated to reflect what was decided.

01 · Department owners
5 of 6 departments locked. HR parked.
Sales & BD: Kenny. Marketing & Content: Kenny (Daunte stepped back). Client Delivery & Design: Eli. AI Workforce & Tech: Daunte (Eli co-builder). Finance & Operations: Daunte. HR & People Ops: parked while Daunte researches role shape.
02 · Decision threshold
$2K splits the rubric
Below $2K with time pressure: dept DRI calls it. Above $2K: full team debate. Rodney's framing: "If you guys think it's a good idea and there's a time constraint, make the call." Kills decision-by-committee on small stuff.
03 · Debate norm
Iron sharpens iron
For big decisions, defaulting to harmony "to keep everything feng shui" is not the desired pattern. Defend the position with rationale. Daunte committed to letting owners move first; team committed to pushing back on Daunte's ideas instead of deferring to "best skillset" framing.
04 · Sales as DNA
Top-of-funnel is everyone's job
Kenny owns the structure (pipeline, HubSpot, lead distribution, deal admin). Top-of-funnel relationship-building lives in BPP's DNA across all owners, not Kenny's lane alone. Rodney's reframe: "establishing the beginnings of relationships, that's not a standalone bucket."
05 · AI agents per dept
Framework central, ownership distributed
AI Workforce & Tech provides the framework (governance, MCP layer, infrastructure, file structure). Each department builds and owns the AI agents that serve its function. Daunte: "The AI workforce is not to own and build every AI employee."
06 · Cross-training for real
Dummy accounts + scheduled sessions
Rodney's litmus test: only Daunte said yes to "could you set up a client in QB + HubSpot + Live Plan alone." Build dummy QB and HubSpot accounts. Daunte leads the transfer sessions. First one: Daunte → Kenny on discovery / sales process, Friday 5/8.
07 · Friday feedback loop
Cellstaff / 15Five model
Short weekly or bi-weekly Friday survey covering each department. Surfaces gaps and continuous-improvement input without waiting for the next big check-in. To be set up post-meeting.
08 · Monthly QC
Data hygiene on a calendar
Monthly review of QuickBooks, HubSpot, and other key systems. Each dept DRI schedules for their domain. AI agents can run scheduled checks. Built into the operating cadence, not an ad-hoc spike.
09 · Wellness norms
Burnout is real. Fun matters. Wins get celebrated.
Rodney drew two lines hard: nobody would do this for free, so it has to be fun enough to sustain. And: BPP has never truly celebrated a client win. Going forward, significant wins get a sit-down. "It'll pay dividends."
10 · Internal sprints
High-leverage only, not routine
"All-hands-on-deck" sprints (LinkedIn campaign, plugin pushes) are reserved for high-leverage, time-bound concentrated effort. Routine automation work happens within a department's normal cadence. Sprint cadence stays case-by-case.

Department Distinction Cheat Sheet

Marketing vs. Design
Marketing is what we SAY (positioning, content, voice, social, newsletter, blog). Design is what we LOOK like (visual identity, logos, brand kit, website appearance, deliverable design).
AI Workforce vs. Finance & Ops
AI Workforce BUILDS technology (plugins, MCPs, automations, hub features). Finance & Ops RUNS the business backbone (QB, AR, SOPs, Drive, vendor mgmt). The plugins Daunte builds = AI Workforce. The QuickBooks book-keeping = Finance.
Finance & Ops vs. HR
Finance & Ops is about MONEY and SYSTEMS (QB, tool stack, vendor mgmt, SOPs, Drive). HR is about PEOPLE and TEAM STRUCTURE (hiring, comp, cross-training, legal/contracts).
Sales & BD vs. Client Delivery
Sales & BD owns the deal from first touch through closed-won. Client Delivery picks up at proposal close: onboarding, project work, status, QC, post-engagement deep-dive.
Tool stack: who decides?
Anyone can propose. Cost gate runs through Finance & Ops (Daunte). Building an integration = AI Workforce. Day-to-day use = wherever the function lives.
Monday board: who owns what?
Monday board STRUCTURE (groups, columns, layout) = Finance & Ops (Daunte/Eli). Monday AUTOMATIONS layer = AI Workforce (Daunte, team-shared over time).
