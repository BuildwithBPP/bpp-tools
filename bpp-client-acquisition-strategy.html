Phone + texting: pick one

All three plug into HubSpot (auto-log texts + calls, send texts from workflows, click-to-call). The real choice is cheap-now vs build-for-a-sales-team. Costs are per user/month, plus about 1¢/text and the carrier registration:

Tool~Cost, 3-4 peopleBest when
OpenPhone / Quo$15-33/user ($45-130/mo)cheapest solid option now (HubSpot on the $33 tier)
Salesmsg$25/mo + $10/seat (~$55-65/mo)simple two-way texting, native HubSpot
Kixie$35-65/user ($105-260/mo)once we add a dialing sales team (it has a power dialer); no switching later

My lean given the cash crunch: start cheap (OpenPhone/Quo or Salesmsg) now, move to Kixie when we actually build the dialer team. Kixie hides its pricing and locks you to annual terms, so get a quote first. HubSpot's own texting add-on is marketing-only, so it doesn't count.

Build this in

Automated follow-up reminders

The #1 reason deals die is forgotten follow-up. Set HubSpot to automatically create the next follow-up task, with a reminder/notification, every time we log a touch. That way the 8-12 touch sequence keeps running on its own and we never have to remember who's due. This is the cadence engine behind the Week 3 volume.

Two setup must-dos

  • Register our business to text customers. Carriers require every business to register before it can send texts from a system like this. It takes ~10-15 days to approve, and our business tax-ID info has to match the IRS records exactly, so start early. The formal name for this is "A2P 10DLC" registration.
  • Move our current business number over (called a "port"): unlock it in Google Voice first, and keep Google Voice running until the move finishes so we never miss a call.

Automation: use what we already have, build only two new things

We already have 63 skills (our pre-built tools). Use or extend the ones for contracts/proposals, revenue operations, customer success, the referral program, pricing, and competitor research, and extend the Monday-prep skill to pull the weekly scoreboard automatically. Build brand-new only two things: a simple lost-deal revival sequence and a case-study builder (folded into our existing closeout-deck skill). Set up the follow-up reminders and an overdue-invoice alert as automatic HubSpot workflows.

08The beachhead: where to point the spear

Chasing every industry at once stops case studies and referrals from compounding. We picked one vertical to focus 90 days on, using research instead of guesswork. Full write-up + sources: beachhead-vertical-research.md

Honest starting point: our own closed deals don't reveal a winning niche. They're scattered, mostly small business-plan work, and our one repeat "cleaning" client (Xtremely Clean) is a single exceptional relationship, not a pattern. We have zero closed automotive deals. So the pick has to come from the market, not our history.

We scored every option on three questions

1. Can they pay?

Does a typical 5-25 person owner have the cash and the pain to buy a $4,500+ build? Strongest: HVAC, pest control, med spas, plumbing.

2. Can we reach them warm?

Dense Tampa associations, owner Facebook groups, and referral partners (insurance brokers, bookkeepers). Strongest: the trades, roofing, cleaning.

3. Can we actually win?

Is the lane open, or already owned by big software plus a wall of agencies and coaches? Most open: cleaning, trucking, commercial landscaping.

The tension that decides it: the verticals that pay best (HVAC, roofing, med spas) are exactly the ones already locked up by dominant software (ServiceTitan, JobNimbus, Mindbody) and a wall of competing agencies and coaches. A two-person local generalist with no vertical proof gets crushed there. The open lanes pay a little less, but we can actually win them.
Lead with this

Commercial cleaning / janitorial

It's the one option that is open, reachable, and where we already have proof. No dominant all-in-one software owns the 5-25 employee B2B cleaner, and almost no agencies compete there, so "we build the whole system" actually lands. We can reach these owners through active owner Facebook groups and bookkeeper / payroll referral partners, and Tampa's B2B cleaning market (offices, medical, hospitality) is thick. And our one real Operator-grade build lives here: Xtremely Clean ($3,315 CRM + automation + sales), with a warm reference. Watch-out: cleaning owners are price-sensitive, so target commercial / recurring-contract cleaners (not solo house cleaners) and anchor on the Operator System.

Second wave

Commercial / HOA landscaping

Adjacent and similar: crews, routes, recurring contracts, and the same insurance-broker and bookkeeper referral graph. Real white space at the 10-25 employee shop that has outgrown the cheap scheduling app but won't pay for enterprise software. Year-round in Tampa, decent margins. Open this once the cleaning proof is compounding.

Don't lead with HVAC, roofing, or med spas right now: best-paying, but most saturated, and we'd be the unknown generalist in a crowded room. We still serve anyone who comes to us; we just point the acquisition spear at one open lane. Source: 3 market-research passes + HubSpot 59-deal review, Jun 2026.

"They cost more per month than our whole system costs once, and they hand you a plan. We hand you a running business."
