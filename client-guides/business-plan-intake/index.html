01 · Setup

Who's in the room

Quick start. Let's make sure we have the basics right before we dig in.

Read aloud
"Let's start with you. Tell me your name, who's on this with you today, and one sentence on what your organization does."
If there's a board chair or co-founder, list them too.
02 · The story

What you actually do

Forget the form language for a minute. We're going to describe what you do the way you'd tell it to a friend.

Read aloud
"Pretend I just met you and asked what you do. How would you explain your business in two sentences?"
03 · Who you serve

The people you help

Walk us through the groups you actually serve. Don't worry about being formal. Paint the picture.

Read aloud
"Picture the person who walks through your door, signs up for your program, or shows up at your event. Who are they? What's going on in their life?"
Primary group The people you most often serve
Second group Optional
Third group Optional
04 · The landscape

Who else is in this space

For a nonprofit, "competitors" usually means peer organizations doing similar work. We want to understand the field, not pick a fight.

Read aloud
"Who else does similar work in your area? Where do they do it well, and where do you think you're tighter or more useful?"
05 · How it runs

The operating side

Now the day-to-day. Who's doing what. What holds it all together.

Read aloud
"Walk me through your team. Who's paid, who's volunteer, who handles what when a Tuesday afternoon goes sideways?"
06 · The money

Funding, budget, and the year ahead

Now the financial side. The prompts below adjust based on the organization type you picked on the cover.

Nonprofit framing
For a 501c3, "investment" really means funding sources (grants, donor pipeline, fiscal sponsor support). "Revenue" really means total funds raised plus earned program revenue. We capture both. The answers feed the same business plan either way.
Read aloud
"Now the money side. We'll go step by step. The big-picture funding ask first, then year-one targets, then what it costs to operate today."
Expense lines Fill what you know. Skip what doesn't apply.
07 · How you grow

Bringing people in and keeping them

"Marketing" is really how people find you, trust you, and stay involved over time.

Read aloud
"How do new customers or partners find you today? And once they're in, what keeps them coming back?"
08 · Looking ahead

The next 12 months

Last stretch. What you're aiming for, what's getting in the way, and where you want our help.

Read aloud
"If we're sitting here again one year from today and it was a great year, what happened?"
